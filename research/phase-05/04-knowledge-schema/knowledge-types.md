## 1. Classification of Design Knowledge

UIForge partitions design knowledge into five distinct functional types:

### 1. `principle` (Invariant Foundations)
- **Definition:** Fundamental laws of human visual perception, optical physics, or motor mechanics that remain invariant across technologies and stylistic eras.
- **Examples:** Gestalt Proximity, F-shaped scanning, Fitts's Law target acquisition, optical line measure.
- **Enforcement:** Evaluated as structural design qualities; violations severely penalize hierarchy and legibility.

### 2. `anti-pattern` (Defect Catalogs)
- **Definition:** Recognizable compositions, layouts, or stylistic tropes that create usability friction, damage readability, violate accessibility, or represent generic/artificial AI templates.
- **Examples:** Repeated centered heroes, low-contrast placeholder text, non-modal focus leaks, desktop layouts squeezed onto mobile.
- **Enforcement:** Triggers automated findings with severity, remediation guidance, and positive counterpart mappings.

### 3. `positive-pattern` (Contextual Solutions)
- **Definition:** Proven, battle-tested layout and interaction architectures optimized for specific domains and user tasks.
- **Examples:** Financial tabular data grid with column pinning, GOV.UK accessible form pattern with error summary, Shopify index table with bulk actions.
- **Enforcement:** Suggested by the Design Intelligence Engine during new project synthesis or existing UI remediation.

### 4. `heuristic` (Empirical Rules of Thumb)
- **Definition:** Practical design conventions derived from enterprise design systems and practitioner experience that yield high-quality results in typical cases, but admit reasoned exceptions.
- **Examples:** 8px spatial grid, limiting primary brand buttons to at most one per viewport, 4:1 heading-to-body scale ratio.
- **Enforcement:** Evaluated contextually; can be overridden if justified by brand requirements or explicit user intent.

### 5. `rule` (Normative Technical Mandates)
- **Definition:** Hard technical constraints imposed by web standards, accessibility laws, or platform specifications.
- **Examples:** WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3 (4.5:1 text contrast), SC 2.5.8 (24px target minimum), WAI-ARIA 1.2 modal focus trapping.
- **Enforcement:** Absolute blocking gates. Cannot be overridden by aesthetic preference.
